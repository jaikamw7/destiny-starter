Your cardio climbs like a staircase

Each workout ends with short climbing rounds — StairMaster or incline treadmill, your pick — with full rest in between. The rounds start small and grow a little each week. Use a slow speed (StairMaster level 3–5, or treadmill at 3.0–3.3 mph on a 5–8% incline).

Things that make this work

🤖 Machines are your friend

They guide the movement so you can work the muscle safely while learning. Adjust the seat so the machine's pivot lines up with the joint that's moving, and do a light first set to find the groove.

🍑 Why glutes get hit 3× a week

Hip thrusts build the main mass, abductions and kickbacks round the sides and lower shelf, RDLs and sumos hit the upper glutes. Different angles, three days, with recovery time between — that's the rounding recipe.

🏋️ Starting weights

Week 1, pick weights that feel almost too easy — finish every set with 2–3 reps left in the tank. Each week after, add a rep or the smallest jump in weight to something. The empty barbell is 45 lbs; that's a real starting weight for hip thrusts (grab a bar pad, usually kept near the benches).

😮‍💨 Five days is a big start

If a week gets busy, dropping to 4 days keeps all your progress — skip Day 5, since its muscles were already trained twice that week. Sore is normal the first couple weeks; sharp or joint pain means stop that exercise and rest.

📅 Consistency beats intensity

Showing up is the entire goal of month one. A short workout where you cut sets still counts as a full win over skipping.

👟 Steps still matter

Gym days don't replace the 10k steps — they stack with them. The steps drive the fat loss; the lifting decides what shape shows up underneath.
